abstract: "It is challenging to implement kernel methods when data sources are distributed and cannot be joined at a trusted third party for privacy reasons. This challenge becomes even more significant when privacy-preserving approaches that introduce noise or high computational overhead are not acceptable, such as in clinical applications. To address this, we propose FLAKE, a framework for learning with anonymized kernels on horizontally distributed data. Our approach enables exact and efficient privacy-preserving computation of kernel methods by allowing data sources to mask their data while still enabling the computation of a Gram matrix. This matrix can be used to train kernel-based machine learning models such as support vector machines without compromising privacy or utility. We show that the framework prevents adversaries from reconstructing the original input data."
