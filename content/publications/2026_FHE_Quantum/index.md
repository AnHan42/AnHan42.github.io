abstract: "Quantum Federated Learning (QFL) enables distributed training of quantum machine learning models by sharing model gradients instead of raw data, but these gradients may still leak sensitive information. To address this, homomorphic encryption has been proposed as a privacy-enhancing technique. In this work, we evaluate the overhead introduced by Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) in QFL settings and assess its feasibility for real-world use. We implement multiple quantum machine learning models, including a quantum convolutional neural network (QCNN), trained in a federated setup with CKKS-encrypted parameters. Our experiments show that memory and communication overhead remain substantial, making practical deployment challenging. Reducing model size alleviates overhead but leads to decreased predictive performance, highlighting a trade-off between privacy and model complexity."
