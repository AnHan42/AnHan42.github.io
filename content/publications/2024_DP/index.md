abstract: "Machine learning is key in modern data analysis, but privacy concerns often limit its application to sensitive data. One widely used technique to ensure privacy is the Private Aggregation of Teacher Ensembles (PATE) framework, which trains an ensemble of teacher models on private data and transfers knowledge to a student model with formal differential privacy guarantees. While PATE has mainly been applied to binary and multi-class classification, many real-world scenarios—especially in medicine—require multi-label predictions. In such settings, a data point may be associated with multiple relevant labels rather than a single class. This paper explores the challenges of applying differentially private learning, and specifically PATE, to multi-label classification tasks, highlighting the increased complexity and trade-offs between privacy and utility."
