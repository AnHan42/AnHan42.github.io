Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) enable direct communication between the brain and external devices, and are used in medical, assistive, and neurofeedback applications. Because brain data is highly sensitive, this project explores federated learning, a decentralised approach that trains models on local devices without sharing raw data, as a way to improve BCI privacy. Data and code from previous BCI experiments are already available to build on.

Tasks include a literature review of federated learning and privacy in BCI systems, setting up the development environment, implementing a federated learning framework tailored to BCI applications, running experiments comparing accuracy, efficiency, and privacy preservation against traditional centralised approaches, and writing up the results. With good results, the outcome could be used as a demonstrator at public events, a basis for future projects, or a joint publication with the supervisors.
